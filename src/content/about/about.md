## The gap we work in

Nearly every company in Switzerland is a small one. Almost none of them are the
size the software industry is built for.

What they get offered instead is a choice between three things. International
software, priced per seat for organisations many times their size and shaped
around somebody else's workflow. An agency, which will do the job properly and
will also hand you an account manager, a minimum project size, and a process
designed for a marketing department. Or the cheapest developer available
remotely, who delivers something that works on the day it is handed over and
then stops answering.

A Zürich language school was in exactly that position — renting scheduling
software priced for an organisation many times its size in order to book six
rooms for eight people. We ran the requirements, made the build-vs-buy call, and
[delivered a replacement they own outright](/projects/sprachschule-yang/). It
went into production in July 2026, two releases have shipped since, and we still
hold the maintenance contract.

That is the gap, and it is not a technical one. The software is rarely hard. The
gap is in who will take a small, specific problem seriously, price it honestly,
and still be there in three years.

## Two people, said plainly

We design and build. That is one practice rather than two departments, and it is
the whole studio — you talk to both of us, and neither of us is a layer between
you and the person doing the work.

One of us is a product engineer: the first conversation, the requirements, the
interface, the build-vs-buy call, and accountability for delivery. The other is
a Swiss senior software engineer with production experience in software that has
to keep running after launch; he implements what we scope and reviews what
ships. Between the two that is design through to infrastructure, which is why
there is no wall in the middle of a project here.

Being two people is the constraint that makes the rest of this possible. We can
take on a project worth a fraction of an agency's minimum, because there is no
agency to pay for. And we cannot disappear behind a team, because there is no
team to disappear behind.

When a build genuinely needs a third pair of hands we bring in a specialist and
say so in the case study. When it is just the two of us, we say which of us did
which half — so you always know who did what either way: see
[who did what on Sprachschule Yang](/projects/sprachschule-yang/).

## Across domains, and across cultures

Two people is only enough if those two people cover ground. Ours cover it in two
directions, and that is how the gap gets filled.

**Across domains.** A problem that arrives as one thing usually turns out to be
several. [A Fatt](/projects/afatt/) arrived as a menu design and was really a
content system, which then became [a piece of software](/projects/menugen/).
[Sprachschule Yang](/projects/sprachschule-yang/) arrived as a room calendar and
was really the record of the hours its teachers are paid on. Research,
interface, product strategy and engineering are not four suppliers here. They
are the same two people, so nothing has to be re-explained when the problem
moves.

**Across cultures.** We work between Switzerland and Asia, and so do our
clients. A Fatt serves Malaysian, Chinese and Taiwanese food in Zürich, on a
menu that has to explain unfamiliar dishes in more than one language;
Sprachschule Yang teaches Chinese and Japanese in Wiedikon. An international
business setting up in Switzerland and a Swiss business selling outward have
the same underlying difficulty: the people who understand the market are rarely
the people who can build the thing. We have sat on both sides of that, which means the first
conversation does not start with explaining the context.

## Why design and build in one place

Most projects lose time and money at the handover between design and
engineering — a specification goes over a wall and comes back as something
adjacent to what was meant. Here there is no wall. The person drawing it and the
person building it are the same two people, in the same conversation, from the
first meeting.

It means a design engagement can turn into working software instead of a
document somebody else has to interpret. A Fatt asked for a menu; the repeating
problem behind it became menuGen. It also means we can tell you when the answer
is *don't build this*, because we know what building it would cost — and one of
us has to maintain it.

## What you can hold us to

- We scope in writing before anyone commits, and the estimate is a number, not a range that moves.
- We say so when buying beats building — even when building is the larger invoice.
- You own what we deliver. The repository and the accounts are yours.
- We hand over things you can maintain without us, and stay on for maintenance when you want us to. Several clients have.
