1. THE HOURS FIGURES DO NOT SURVIVE ARITHMETIC.  CLOSED — kept as-is.
   Recorded because the analysis cost something and may be wanted later;
   not because anything is waiting on it.
   hours-admin.png reports September 2026 as 590 lessons / 880 hours
   across eight people:

     Ada     130 lessons  195h      Nina    95   142h30
     Ayende   91          136h30    Luca    85   127h30
     Elio     82          123h      Lena    77   115h30

   Ada's 195h is 8.9 contact hours every working day, with no gap
   between lessons. Five other people sit above 115h. These are
   contact hours, not working hours; nobody teaches this much.

   Anyone who has run a language school does that sum in seconds,
   and they do it on the page whose punchline is "it will not print
   a number it cannot stand behind". It is the worst available place
   to display a number nobody could.

   VERIFIED AGAINST THE DATABASE, 2026-09-01: 581 bookings, 136
   recurring series, ZERO one-offs, running Mon-Fri already
   (Sat 2, Sun 0). The problem is not weekend scheduling. It is
   density per working day.

   ⚠️ "Just shoot a one-week range instead" does NOT work.
   195h/month is ~45h/week — a weekly view shows the same
   implausible load in smaller numbers. The data has to change.

   FIX: thin whole SERIES, never individual lessons. Dropping single
   occurrences leaves a weekly course with holes in it, which reads
   as a bug in the calendar shots. Dropping every second series per
   teacher lands the top teacher near 97h (~4.4h per working day)
   and the total near 430h.

   The script is written: fix-demo-hours.sql, in this session's
   scratchpad. It confirms the org first, backs up, previews before
   deleting, and carries a restore path.

   ⛔ IT HAS NOT BEEN RUN. Testing Org shares a production database
   with Sprachschule Yang's real schedule
   (9629ea1c-8930-4467-93f4-943c2f8123fa — never touch), so it wants

PART 2 — THE CALL: STILLS, PLUS ONE SHORT SILENT LOOP
============================================================
Not a narrated walkthrough. Not a product tour.

  a) The app is behind a login, so there is no "try it" link. Media
     is the only proof there is — which argues for proof that can be
     checked at a glance, not proof that has to be watched.
  b) The page is an argument, read by someone deciding whether to
     hire a studio. Stills sit inside the sentence they support;
     a video sits outside it.
  c) A tour invites feature comparison — "does it also do X?" — which
     is the frame this case study argues against. The page says *we
     counted what you actually use*.

Exactly one claim cannot be photographed. That one gets a loop.

PART 4 — THE ONE VIDEO
============================================================
The refusal is a toast: it appears, names what clashed, disappears.
A still of a toast reads as an error someone hit, not a rule the
system enforces. Worse for a series, where it names the dates —
"Conflicts on: Sep 10, Sep 17, Sep 24" — the system checking a whole
term at once, and completely invisible frozen.

  CHECK FIRST: does video/newbooking.mov already contain a rejected
  booking? If not, this is the one recording still missing, and it is
  the only one the page actually needs.

  The take — 15–25s, silent, looping:
    1. Day view, owner. A booked slot clearly visible.   (2s)
    2. New booking → pick that room, that time.          (5s)
    3. Save → the refusal, naming the clash.             (3s)
    4. Move to a free slot → Save → it lands.            (6s)
    5. Hold on the finished day, then loop.              (2s)
  Use a recurring series if it is easy to stage: the refusal that
  names three dates is a better three seconds.

  ENCODE: H.264 MP4 + VP9 WebM, 1280px wide, no audio track at all
  (not a silent one), under 1.5MB. Poster frame from step 1.

  ⚠️ DO NOT SHIP THE GIF OR THE ANIMATED WEBP.
  gif/iphone.gif is 20MB and webp/iphone.webp is 9MB — for one
  clip, on one page. The .mov sources in video/ encode to MP4/WebM at
  a tenth to a twentieth of that with better colour. GIF is a 1987
  format with a 256-colour palette; it will band the calendar's
  teacher colours, which are the one thing that has to stay legible.
